South Sudan Launches First National Cancer Registry in Juba

Published: 7 November 2026 | Vivitek Health News

Officials from the Ministry of Health, partners and doctors attend the launch of the National Cancer Registry Centre in Juba. Photograph: Awan Achiek / The Dawn Newspaper. Used with permission.

JUBA, South Sudan — South Sudan has launched its first National Population-Based Cancer Registry at Juba Teaching Hospital, marking an important step towards improving the country's understanding of cancer and strengthening health planning.

The initiative, launched on 5 November 2026, is intended to help collect, analyse and report information about cancer cases, including the types of cancer affecting communities, their distribution and treatment outcomes.

Addressing the cancer data gap

Health authorities have highlighted the lack of reliable national cancer data as a major challenge. Without consistent information, it is difficult to understand the scale of the disease, identify affected populations and plan appropriate health services.

Caretaker Health Minister Dak Doup Bichok said the country had previously lacked reliable national information on cancer. The new registry is expected to help address this gap by establishing a more organised system for collecting information from health facilities.

Thousands affected by cancer

Estimates cited during reporting on the launch indicate that South Sudan faces a substantial cancer burden. Figures attributed to international cancer estimates include approximately 8,124 new cases and 5,128 cancer-related deaths.

These figures are modelled estimates, not a complete count of patients recorded by the newly launched registry. Establishing reliable local records will help health authorities build a clearer picture of the disease over time.

How the registry is expected to work

Registry director Dr Albino Amum said trained personnel would collect information from public and private hospitals, clinics, health centres and other healthcare facilities.

The registry is initially based at Juba Teaching Hospital. Information gathered from participating facilities is expected to support national reporting and provide evidence for health policy and planning.

Challenges that must be addressed

The success of the initiative will depend on sustained funding, trained cancer registration personnel, cooperation between healthcare facilities and reliable reporting systems.

Protecting patient confidentiality and ensuring that information is accurate will also be essential. A registry can provide valuable evidence, but it does not itself diagnose or treat cancer.

Turning information into better healthcare

Reliable cancer data can help the government and its partners identify priority needs, plan services, strengthen prevention programmes and improve access to early diagnosis and treatment.

For patients and families, the longer-term value of the registry will depend on whether the information collected leads to practical improvements in cancer awareness, diagnosis, treatment and supportive care.

South Sudan's new registry therefore represents an important beginning. Its lasting impact will depend on consistent implementation and the use of evidence to improve health services across the country.
