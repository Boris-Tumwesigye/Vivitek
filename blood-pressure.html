Understanding Blood Pressure: History, Measurement and Its Importance

Published: October 30, 2026

Health Education | Vivitek

Introduction

Blood pressure is one of the most important measurements used in healthcare. It measures the force exerted by circulating blood against the walls of the arteries. Maintaining healthy blood pressure is essential for protecting the heart, brain, kidneys and other vital organs.

The History of Blood Pressure

The understanding of blood pressure developed over several centuries.

In 1733, English scientist Stephen Hales performed the first recorded direct measurement of arterial blood pressure in a horse. His experiment involved inserting a tube into an artery and observing how high blood rose in the tube.

In 1896, Italian physician Scipione Riva-Rocci introduced an inflatable cuff-based blood pressure measuring device. This invention made blood pressure measurement more practical.

In 1905, Russian surgeon Nikolai Korotkoff described the sounds heard through a stethoscope when measuring blood pressure. These became known as Korotkoff sounds and remain important in manual blood pressure measurement today.

What Is Blood Pressure?

Blood pressure is recorded using two numbers:

  • Systolic pressure: The pressure when the heart contracts and pumps blood.
  • Diastolic pressure: The pressure when the heart relaxes between beats.

For example, a reading of 120/80 mmHg means a systolic pressure of 120 and a diastolic pressure of 80 millimetres of mercury.

Understanding Blood Pressure Readings

  • Normal adult blood pressure: Below 120/80 mmHg.
  • Elevated blood pressure: Systolic 120–129 and diastolic below 80 mmHg.
  • High blood pressure: Persistent readings of 130/80 mmHg or higher under American Heart Association classification.

Blood pressure categories vary slightly between clinical guidelines. A single reading does not establish a diagnosis.

Causes and Risk Factors of High Blood Pressure

  • Excessive salt consumption.
  • Physical inactivity.
  • Excess body weight.
  • Smoking and excessive alcohol consumption.
  • Family history of hypertension.
  • Increasing age.
  • Some kidney conditions and medications.

Signs and Symptoms

High blood pressure is often called a silent condition because many people experience no symptoms. The only reliable way to identify it is through proper blood pressure measurement.

How Is Blood Pressure Measured?

Blood pressure can be measured using a manual sphygmomanometer and stethoscope or an automated digital blood pressure monitor.

For a more accurate measurement:

  1. Avoid smoking, caffeine and exercise for 30 minutes beforehand.
  2. Sit quietly for at least five minutes.
  3. Keep your back supported and feet flat on the floor.
  4. Support your arm at heart level.
  5. Use the correct cuff size.
  6. Take two readings about one minute apart.

Prevention and Control

  • Eat a balanced diet rich in fruits and vegetables.
  • Reduce salt intake.
  • Exercise regularly.
  • Maintain a healthy body weight.
  • Avoid tobacco.
  • Limit alcohol consumption.
  • Monitor blood pressure regularly.
  • Follow prescribed treatment and attend medical appointments.

Possible Complications

Uncontrolled hypertension increases the risk of stroke, heart attack, heart failure, kidney disease and damage to blood vessels.

Conclusion

Blood pressure measurement has evolved from early scientific experiments to modern digital monitoring devices. Today, regular monitoring, healthy lifestyle choices and appropriate medical treatment help prevent complications and save lives.
