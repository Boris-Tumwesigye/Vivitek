KCCA Rescues 114 Children from Kampala Streets: Where Did They Come From?

By Vivitek News Desk | 9 November 2026 | Uganda News

The Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA) says it has rescued 114 children from the streets of Kampala in an operation conducted on Friday evening, 6 November 2026.

According to reports of the operation, the group comprised 82 girls and 32 boys. The children were reportedly taken to UWESO Masulita Children's Village in Wakiso District as efforts to trace their families continue.

Where Did the Children Come From?

One important question following the operation is where the rescued children originally came from.

KCCA has not yet published a confirmed breakdown showing the home districts or villages of the 114 children. Previous concerns raised by authorities about children living on Kampala's streets have highlighted the movement of vulnerable children from other parts of Uganda into the capital.

Some children may arrive in Kampala with relatives or other adults, while others may be attracted by promises of employment, education or better living conditions. However, the circumstances of each child must be established individually.

It would therefore be premature to attribute the 114 children to particular districts without confirmation from KCCA or the relevant authorities.

Children Face Risks on the Streets

Children living on the streets can face serious risks, including abuse, exploitation, inadequate food, unsafe accommodation and limited access to healthcare and education.

Authorities and child-protection organisations have an important role in identifying children at risk, investigating suspected exploitation and ensuring that children receive appropriate protection.

Care and Family Tracing

The children were reportedly taken to UWESO Masulita Children's Village in Wakiso District. Care, assessment and family tracing are important steps in determining what support each child needs.

Where it is safe and appropriate, family reunification can help children return to stable home environments. Other children may require longer-term protection and support.

A Wider Child Protection Challenge

Rescuing children from the streets is only one part of addressing the problem. Sustainable solutions also require family support, access to education, community awareness and action against anyone who exploits children.

Local authorities, families, police and child-protection organisations need to work together to prevent children from being drawn into unsafe street environments.

What Happens Next?

The next important step is to establish the identities and home communities of the rescued children, trace their parents or guardians and assess the circumstances that brought them to Kampala.

For now, the individual origins of the 114 children have not been publicly confirmed. Further information from KCCA will be important in establishing those details.

Sources

This report is based on information attributed to KCCA and reporting by Uganda Broadcasting Corporation and Daily Monitor on 7 November 2026.

Vivitek will update this report if authorities release further verified information about the children's home districts, family tracing or rehabilitation.
