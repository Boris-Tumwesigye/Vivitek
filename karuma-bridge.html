Uganda Begins Construction of New Karuma Bridge: A Major Boost for Northern Uganda and Regional Trade

By Vivitek News Desk

Uganda has commenced construction of a new bridge across the Victoria Nile at Karuma, marking an important development in the country's transport infrastructure and regional connectivity.

The project is intended to replace the ageing Karuma Bridge, constructed in 1963, which serves as a critical transport connection between central Uganda, northern Uganda and neighbouring countries.

Where did it happen?

The project is located at Karuma, where the Victoria Nile separates Nwoya District from Kiryandongo District in Uganda.

The crossing is strategically positioned along the Kampala–Gulu highway and serves motorists, commercial transporters and traders travelling between Uganda and South Sudan.

When did construction begin?

According to information released by Uganda's Ministry of Works and Transport, civil construction works commenced on 1 October 2026. The project was publicly announced on 4 November 2026.

Japanese construction company Zenitaka Corporation is responsible for carrying out the works, while Oriental Consultants Global is providing consultancy services. The project is expected to take approximately 36 months.

Why is Uganda constructing another bridge?

The existing Karuma Bridge has experienced longstanding safety and structural challenges. Its narrow carriageway and sharp bends along the approach roads have created difficulties for motorists, particularly drivers transporting heavy commercial goods.

The government has moved ahead with a replacement project intended to improve road safety and accommodate increasing transport demands.

How will the project benefit Uganda and South Sudan?

  • Improve road safety for motorists and passengers.
  • Support the movement of commercial goods.
  • Strengthen trade between Uganda and South Sudan.
  • Improve connectivity between central and northern Uganda.
  • Support access to tourism destinations, including Murchison Falls National Park.

The project is being implemented with support from the Japanese government through the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA).

What happens to the old bridge?

The existing bridge is expected to remain operational during construction, according to information reported by Ugandan media. This arrangement is intended to maintain connectivity while the new infrastructure is developed.

What happens next?

Construction is expected to continue over approximately 36 months. Once completed, the new bridge is expected to provide a more reliable transport connection across the Victoria Nile.

Vivitek Perspective

Infrastructure development is not only about building roads and bridges. It is also about improving people's access to markets, healthcare, education and economic opportunities.

The new Karuma Bridge could become an important investment in regional trade and connectivity between Uganda and South Sudan.

Sources: Uganda Ministry of Works and Transport information and Ugandan media reporting.
