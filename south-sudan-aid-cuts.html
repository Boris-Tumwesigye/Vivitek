South Sudan: Aid Cuts Put 150,000 Displaced People at Risk of Disease, MSF Warns

By Vivitek Health Desk | Published: 6 November 2026

JUBA, South Sudan — Approximately 150,000 displaced people living in Bentiu and Malakal are facing increased health risks following reductions in humanitarian funding and the withdrawal of aid organisations, according to Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF).

The medical humanitarian organisation warns that reduced access to clean water, sanitation and essential healthcare is increasing vulnerability to cholera and other waterborne diseases.

Serious sanitation challenges in Bentiu

Bentiu displacement camp in Unity State hosts approximately 111,000 people, according to MSF.

An assessment conducted by the organisation found that around 60 people share one functioning latrine, while only 109 handwashing stations serve the camp.

The camp is also located in a flood-prone area, increasing concerns about sanitation and water contamination.

MSF reports that the International Organization for Migration (IOM) is currently the only organisation providing basic healthcare and water, sanitation and hygiene services in the camp.

Funding for water and sanitation services has been temporarily extended until March 2027. However, continuation beyond that period remains uncertain without additional support. Basic healthcare services face a major funding gap from January 2027.

Malakal faces water supply concerns

In Malakal, Upper Nile State, funding shortages are also threatening essential services.

MSF warns that without additional funding, water supplies serving approximately 40,000 people could stop. The emptying of around 900 latrines could also be disrupted.

Healthcare services have contracted following the withdrawal of the International Medical Corps, which previously supported outpatient consultations, laboratory services, mental healthcare and routine immunisation.

Local health authorities face shortages of medical supplies and healthcare workers, making it difficult to replace the withdrawn services.

South Sudan continues to face cholera risks

MSF reports that South Sudan recorded more than 14,000 cholera cases between January and September 2026.

Rubkona County in Unity State, where Bentiu is located, accounted for nearly one-third of the reported cases during that period, according to the organisation.

Working with the Ministry of Health, MSF vaccinated 102,251 people during the second round of oral cholera vaccination in Bentiu in September 2026, reaching approximately 75 per cent of the targeted population.

The organisation stresses that vaccination must be accompanied by safe drinking water, functioning latrines and adequate sanitation to prevent future outbreaks.

Humanitarian organisations call for urgent support

MSF is calling on donors and humanitarian organisations to provide additional funding to maintain essential healthcare, water supply, sanitation and hygiene services.

The organisation says that changes in the administrative status of displacement camps do not remove the humanitarian needs of the people living there.

Many displaced families remain unable to return safely to their places of origin.

Why this matters

The situation in Bentiu and Malakal highlights the connection between humanitarian assistance and public health.

Limited access to safe water, inadequate sanitation and reduced healthcare services can increase the risk of infectious disease outbreaks, particularly among vulnerable displaced communities.

Maintaining disease surveillance, vaccination, clean water and sanitation is important not only for South Sudan but also for wider regional public health preparedness.

Important clarification: This report concerns increased disease risks identified by MSF. It does not announce a newly confirmed cholera outbreak in either camp.
