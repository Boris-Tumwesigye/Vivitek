Uganda Reports Progress Against Malaria and HIV, but Healthcare Financing Remains a Challenge

By Vivitek Health Desk · 7 November 2026 · Health

Uganda's latest health-sector performance report has highlighted improvements in malaria and HIV prevention, alongside continuing challenges affecting healthcare delivery across the country.

The Annual Health Sub-Programme Performance Report for the 2025/2026 financial year was launched on 4 November 2026 by Health Minister Dr Chris Baryomunsi at Speke Resort Munyonyo in Kampala.

Improvements in disease control

According to figures reported from the Ministry of Health performance report, malaria incidence declined from 261 to 223 cases per 1,000 people.

HIV incidence also fell from 1.23 to 0.71 per 1,000 people, while tuberculosis treatment success improved from 86% to 92%.

Key health indicators
  • Malaria incidence: 261 to 223 per 1,000 people.
  • HIV incidence: 1.23 to 0.71 per 1,000 people.
  • TB treatment success: 86% to 92%.
  • Institutional maternal mortality: 83 to 61.7 per 100,000 deliveries.

Healthcare financing remains a concern

Despite the improvements, the report highlights continuing financial and human-resource challenges.

The health sector's share of the national budget declined from 7.7% to 6.2%, while out-of-pocket healthcare expenditure increased from 28% to 30.3%.

Public-sector staffing also remained at approximately 34% of the approved staffing structure.

Hospital deaths raise further concerns

Daily Monitor separately reported an increase in recorded inpatient deaths, from 47,269 in 2023 to 50,563 in 2025.

These are reported inpatient figures and should not be interpreted as a national population mortality rate. The reported increase does not by itself establish the reasons behind the change.

Why the findings matter

Uganda's progress in disease control is important for families, health workers and neighbouring countries, including South Sudan.

Continued investment in healthcare financing, staffing, essential medicines and early diagnosis will be important in sustaining improvements.
