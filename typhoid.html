Typhoid Fever: Symptoms, Prevention and Treatment

Typhoid fever is a bacterial infection caused by Salmonella Typhi. It usually spreads through food or water contaminated with the bacteria. Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment help reduce the risk of serious complications.

What Are the Symptoms of Typhoid?

Symptoms commonly develop 6–30 days after exposure and may include:

  • Persistent fever that may rise over several days
  • Headache and weakness
  • Stomach pain and loss of appetite
  • Constipation or diarrhoea
  • Nausea and sometimes vomiting

These symptoms can also occur with other illnesses, including malaria. Symptoms alone cannot confirm typhoid.

Abdominal Pain in Typhoid Fever: Causes, Treatment and Warning Signs

Abdominal pain is a common symptom of typhoid fever. It may occur alongside persistent fever, headache, weakness, loss of appetite, constipation or diarrhoea.

Typhoid fever is caused by Salmonella Typhi, a bacterium usually transmitted through food or water contaminated by an infected person's faeces. However, abdominal pain does not automatically mean someone has typhoid. Other infections and medical conditions can cause similar symptoms.

Why Does Typhoid Cause Abdominal Pain?

The infection affects the digestive system, particularly the intestines. This can cause abdominal discomfort or pain. Some patients also experience nausea, bloating, constipation or diarrhoea.

Severe intestinal complications can develop in some untreated or advanced cases. These may include intestinal bleeding or perforation.

How Is Abdominal Pain in Typhoid Treated?

Treatment focuses on controlling the infection and managing any complications.

  • Medical assessment: A healthcare professional should assess persistent fever and abdominal pain and recommend appropriate tests.
  • Antibiotics: Typhoid is treated with antibiotics selected by a qualified healthcare professional, taking local antibiotic resistance into account.
  • Fluids: Drink safe water and use oral rehydration solution when appropriate, especially if diarrhoea or vomiting occurs.
  • Follow-up: Complete prescribed treatment and return for medical review if symptoms persist or worsen.

Blood culture is an important test for diagnosing typhoid. The Widal test alone should not be relied upon because false-positive results can occur.

Warning Signs That Need Urgent Medical Attention

Seek urgent medical care if someone with suspected or confirmed typhoid develops:

  • Sudden, severe or worsening abdominal pain.
  • A swollen or very tender abdomen.
  • Blood in the stool or black, tar-like stools.
  • Vomiting blood.
  • Fainting, confusion or severe weakness.
  • Persistent vomiting or signs of severe dehydration.

These symptoms may indicate serious complications and should not be managed at home.

Important: Do not take leftover antibiotics or choose antibiotics without medical advice. The correct treatment depends on the diagnosis and local antibiotic resistance patterns.

How Is Typhoid Diagnosed?

How Is Typhoid Treated?

Typhoid is treated with antibiotics selected by a healthcare professional, taking local antibiotic resistance into account. Do not self-prescribe antibiotics. Drink safe fluids and follow your clinician's instructions.

Seek urgent medical care for severe abdominal pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, bleeding, or signs of dehydration.

How Can Typhoid Be Prevented?

  • Drink treated, boiled or otherwise safe water.
  • Wash hands with soap after using the toilet and before handling food.
  • Cook food thoroughly and eat it while hot.
  • Wash fruit and vegetables with safe water.
  • Keep food and drinking water protected from contamination.
  • Ask a healthcare professional whether typhoid vaccination is recommended.

When Should You Seek Medical Help?

Seek medical assessment for a persistent fever, especially after consuming unsafe food or water. Prompt testing is important because fever can have several causes, including malaria.
