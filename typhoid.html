Typhoid Fever: Symptoms, Prevention and Treatment Typhoid fever is a bacterial infection caused by Salmonella Typhi. It usually spreads through food or water contaminated with the bacteria. Typhoid can be serious, but early diagnosis and appropriate treatment can help prevent complications. What Are the Symptoms of Typhoid? Symptoms commonly develop 6–30 days after exposure and may include: - Persistent fever that may rise over several days - Headache and weakness - Stomach pain - Loss of appetite - Constipation or diarrhoea - Nausea and sometimes vomiting Symptoms can overlap with malaria and other infections. You cannot reliably confirm typhoid from symptoms alone. How Is Typhoid Diagnosed? A healthcare professional may request blood cultures or other appropriate tests. Blood culture is an important diagnostic test. Some rapid tests can be unreliable, so test results should be interpreted by a qualified health professional. How Is Typhoid Treated? Typhoid is treated with antibiotics selected by a healthcare professional, taking local antibiotic resistance into account. Do not self-prescribe antibiotics or stop a prescribed course without medical advice. Drink safe fluids and follow your clinician’s instructions. Seek urgent medical care for severe abdominal pain, confusion, persistent vomiting, bleeding, or signs of dehydration. How Can Typhoid Be Prevented? - Drink treated, boiled or otherwise safe water. - Wash hands with soap after using the toilet and before preparing or eating food. - Cook food thoroughly and eat it while hot. - Wash fruit and vegetables with safe water. - Keep food and drinking water protected from contamination. - Consider typhoid vaccination where recommended by a healthcare professional. When Should You Seek Medical Help? Seek medical assessment if you have a persistent fever, especially after consuming unsafe food or water or travelling in an area where typhoid occurs. Fever can have several causes, including malaria, so prompt testing is important.
