Ebola Disease: Symptoms, Transmission and Prevention

Ebola disease is a rare but severe infectious illness caused by viruses in the orthoebolavirus group. It can cause serious illness and requires prompt medical attention. Early supportive care improves the chances of survival.

What Are the Symptoms of Ebola?

Symptoms may begin between 2 and 21 days after infection. Early symptoms can include:

  • Sudden fever
  • Severe tiredness or weakness
  • Headache
  • Muscle and body pain
  • Sore throat

Other symptoms may follow, including vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain and rash. Bleeding can occur but is not present in every case.

How Does Ebola Spread?

Ebola can spread through direct contact with the blood or other body fluids of a person who is sick or has died from the disease. It can also spread through objects contaminated with infected body fluids.

Infection can occur when these fluids enter the body through broken skin or the eyes, nose or mouth. People are not considered infectious before symptoms begin.

How Can Ebola Be Prevented?

  • Avoid direct contact with the body fluids of anyone suspected or confirmed to have Ebola.
  • Wash your hands regularly with soap and safe water.
  • Do not handle the body of someone who may have died from Ebola. Contact trained health authorities for safe and dignified burial arrangements.
  • Avoid touching or eating animals found dead or sick, and follow public-health advice about wildlife and bushmeat.
  • Healthcare workers should follow recommended infection-prevention procedures and use appropriate protective equipment.
  • Follow vaccination advice from health authorities during an outbreak. Available vaccines do not protect against every Ebola-related virus.

What Should You Do If You Suspect Ebola?

If you develop compatible symptoms after possible exposure, or have had direct contact with someone suspected of having Ebola, contact a health facility or local public-health authority immediately for instructions.

Call ahead before travelling to a health facility, avoid close contact with others, and follow instructions from trained health workers. Do not attempt to care for a suspected case at home without professional guidance.

Important: Ebola symptoms can resemble malaria, typhoid and other illnesses. Symptoms alone cannot confirm Ebola. Laboratory testing and assessment by trained health professionals are needed.
